2 Due Fri 6 Nov

Structured data

The site has no structured data today. We'll add six blocks, built only from what's already on the site.

In plain words

Structured data is a hidden label on each page that tells Google and AI tools exactly what they're looking at: who Troopod is, what it sells and how the pages fit together. Visitors don't see it. Search engines use it for richer results and to build Troopod's brand profile.

LabelPageWhat it tells Google
Organization + WebSiteEvery pageWho Troopod is: legal name, logo, Mumbai address, email, LinkedIn and Instagram, founders
Service/The five solutions Troopod sells
Service + Breadcrumb/agencyTrooLaunch, the offer for agencies
CollectionPage + Breadcrumb/case-studiesThis page is a set of client results
FAQPage/founderslanderThe page's three questions and answers
Breadcrumb/privacy, /terms, /refundCleaner page paths in search results

What we're not adding, and why

  • Review stars: Google doesn't show stars for reviews a business publishes about itself, and it can penalise them.
  • LocalBusiness: Troopod has no walk-in office.
  • Prices: the site has no public pricing.
  • Advisors as employees: that would be inaccurate.

Open questions

Keyword research and the keyword tree

We'll pick 3–4 brand keywords that work as synonyms for the name Troopod. Each one belongs to one of the four main pages.

In plain words

A keyword tree is a family tree for search terms. The brand sits at the top. Under it are a few brand keywords, under each of those are the keywords for one page, and blog posts hang off the bottom. Every keyword has exactly one home, so our own pages never compete with each other.

How the brand keywords get used

Once chosen, they appear next to the name everywhere: "Troopod, the AI CRO agency". That means title tags, the structured data description, the About copy, social bios and link text.

How we'll choose them

Relevant

It describes what Troopod actually sells.

Searched

Enough people in India and the US look for it.

Winnable

troopod.io can realistically rank for it in 6–12 months.

Buyer intent

The person searching wants to hire or buy, not just learn.

Sources: Google Search Console (what troopod.io and the blog already rank for), Google Keyword Planner, and Ahrefs or Semrush for volume and difficulty.

4 After keywords are chosen

Map and optimise existing content

Every page gets 3–5 keywords from the tree. No keyword is targeted by more than one page.

In plain words

If two of our pages chase the same keyword, Google has to guess which one to show, and both end up ranking lower. Giving each keyword one page avoids that.

  1. List every page that search engines can see, plus each blog post once it moves over.
  2. Give each page one main keyword and 2–4 supporting ones from the tree.
  3. Check the full list: every keyword appears on one page only.
  4. Rewrite each page's title, description, headings, opening copy and image text to use its keywords naturally.
  5. Update the page's date in the sitemap.

5 After content mapping

Internal linking to the four main pages

Every supporting page and blog post links up to the main page that owns its topic, so their authority flows to those four pages.

In plain words

Each link from one of our pages to another passes along some of its ranking power. Pointing those links at the four main pages builds them up.

Supporting pages
Growth guides blog
How-to posts blog
Result breakdowns blog
Agency playbooks blog
→
Main pages
Homepage troopod.io/
Solutions troopod.io/#solutions
Case studies troopod.io/case-studies
For agencies troopod.io/agency

The rules

  • Each blog post links to one main page, the one whose keywords match it, early in the post.
  • The link text is that page's keyword, never "click here" or "learn more".
  • The four main pages link to each other, and each links down to 3–5 of its best posts.
  • Posts on the same topic link to each other, so each group holds together.
  • No page links to a main page using a keyword that belongs to a different main page.
  • Header and footer links count, but links inside the body copy count more.
6 After keywords are chosen

Move the blog onto troopod.io

The blog's 140 posts live on a separate subdomain, so they build little authority for troopod.io itself. Moving them under troopod.io/blog fixes that.

Today
blog.troopod.io
301 redirect →
After
troopod.io/blog
In plain words

Google treats blog.troopod.io as mostly a different website. Once the posts sit at troopod.io/blog, all their links and readers count toward Troopod's main site, and step 5 can send that strength to the four main pages.

  1. Change the blog's address setting to troopod.io/blog. The blog runs on Ghost 6.24, which supports this.
  2. Route troopod.io/blog to the blog on the web server. Both sites already run on nginx, possibly on the same machine.
  3. Redirect every old blog.troopod.io address to its new troopod.io/blog address (a 301), so rankings and backlinks carry over.
  4. Point the "Blogs" links in the site header and footer to /blog.
  5. Add the blog's posts to the sitemap.
  6. Check Google Search Console for errors over the next 4–6 weeks.
  7. Apply steps 4 and 5 to the posts, starting with the ones that get the most traffic.
